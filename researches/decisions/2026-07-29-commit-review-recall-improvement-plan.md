# Commit-Review Recall Improvement Plan

**Proposed destination:** `researches/decisions/2026-07-29-commit-review-recall-improvement-plan.md`

## 1. Decision frame

### Objective

Raise commit-review **final-surviving recall** without increasing false-positive or coordinator noise.

### Constraints

- Preserve the coordinator/disposition layer. The S3 probe found **0 of 39** dropped blind-spot emissions to be real findings, so there is no evidence that disposition is suppressing valid findings.
- Do not increase blind-spot detection volume. The examined blind-spot volume was noise, not latent recall.
- Preserve **INFORMS-only** leaf authority.
- Keep core changes domain-free and adopter-neutral.
- Do not increase aggregate panel-seat count.
- Evaluate final-surviving findings, not raw emissions.
- Use aggregate statistics and neutral paraphrases only.
- Stop before implementation or exact prompt wording.

### Success criteria

1. A statistically and operationally credible increase in final-surviving recall.
2. Stable or improved precision and positive-verification noise rate.
3. No change to disposition authority or coordinator semantics.
4. Low-cost, reversible interventions precede expensive or model-dependent ones.
5. Every adopted lever has a predeclared falsifier and rollback condition.

### Open question resolved

The preferred sequence is:

> **A → E → B → F → D**, with **C restricted to a residual, precision-gated shadow experiment**.

This ordering optimizes the qualitative ratio:

> **expected recall gain × 1 / (noise risk × operational/context cost)**

The bucket percentages are target populations, not recoverable-gain forecasts, and must not be added together.

## 4. Sequenced plan

## Stage 1 — A: expression hygiene

### Why first

A targets the largest harness-fixable bucket, has the lowest implementation and context cost, is largely model-independent, and is independently supported by both sides of the evidence:

- real concerns were sometimes noticed but not expressed as findings;
- positive-verification notes create substantial non-finding output.

It therefore has the best expected gain-to-risk-to-cost ratio.

### Intended behavior

The shared leaf instructions should distinguish:

1. a material concern, which must be emitted as a structured, severitied finding with concrete evidence;
2. a tentative observation lacking sufficient evidence, which must not be inflated into a finding;
3. a successful verification, which should normally be omitted rather than emitted as review chatter.

This is expression hygiene, not a request to discover or emit more finding classes.

### Gate

Advance only if final-surviving recall rises without a proportional rise in low-severity, unsupported, duplicate, or positive-verification emissions.

---

## Stage 2 — E: stakes-based seat allocation

### Why second

E addresses the other major mechanism family—the 39.1% capability-bound share—without adding seats or increasing raw review volume. It exploits the measured seat capability ordering rather than assuming equal performance across classes.

E precedes B despite being model-dependent because:

- it targets a materially larger bucket;
- contract-related hard cases are disproportionately capability-bound;
- it can be implemented as a reversible allocation change at fixed panel cost;
- B’s direct opportunity is smaller and its broader benefit is partly diagnostic.

### Intended behavior

Use the strongest seat for the highest-stakes or empirically capability-bound review classes. Preserve at least one independently attributable seat rather than concentrating every review on one evaluator.

The routing policy must be domain-free and expressed in generic review terms such as declared-contract consistency, state transitions, data invariants, or subtle concurrency correctness.

### Gate

Advance only if fixed-seat-count routing improves final-surviving recall on the targeted slices without reducing overall precision or eliminating useful independent corroboration.

---

## Stage 3 — B: changed-unit accounting

### Why third

B directly targets the 8.7% skim bucket and provides diagnostic value: it can distinguish “the leaf examined this unit and missed the issue” from “the leaf did not substantively examine this unit.”

It precedes F because it has lower noise risk. F asks leaves to consider additional obligations; B first improves evidence about where their existing attention went.

### Intended behavior

Each leaf should concisely account for every changed unit using a bounded status such as:

- examined, no actionable concern;
- examined, finding emitted;
- not substantively examined, with reason.

This accounting remains advisory. It must not automatically create findings, blocks, or coordinator transitions.

For large changes, accounting should use meaningful changed units rather than mechanically repeating every diff hunk.

### Gate

Advance only if known skim misses become either correctly reviewed or explicitly visible as unexamined, without significant output growth or attention degradation on already-covered units.

---

## Stage 4 — F: narrow prompt-gap closure

### Why fourth

The prompt-gap bucket is larger than the skim bucket, but changing review obligations has a higher risk of soliciting speculative findings. It should follow A so that any new obligation inherits stricter expression discipline, and follow B so that failures of attention can be distinguished from failures of obligation.

### Intended behavior

Add one demonstrated, high-stakes obligation at a time. Each obligation must:

- correspond to upheld historical findings rather than emitted volume;
- state a materiality and evidence bar;
- remain domain-free;
- avoid broad encouragement to speculate;
- exclude categories for which additional emissions were already shown to be noise.

The observed blast-radius, performance, and concurrency gaps establish that same-prompt seat substitution does not solve prompt omissions. They do **not** authorize an unbounded request to emit more findings in those categories.

### Gate

Advance one obligation only when it creates net-new final-surviving findings at a stable precision floor. Remove any obligation that mainly increases dropped or unsupported emissions.

---

## Stage 5 — D: selective context expansion

### Why last

D addresses the smallest measured bucket, **4.3%**, while carrying the highest context tax. General expansion could also worsen skim behavior by diluting attention.

### Intended behavior

Supply only the minimum additional context necessary for a declared contract, invariant, or dormant surface implicated by the changed unit. Context should be bounded and provenance-marked.

Do not make full-function, full-contract, or dormant-surface expansion the default for every change. Trigger it only where a deterministic selection rule indicates that the additional context is relevant.

### Gate

Adopt only if the residual miss population still contains a measurable context-absent class after A–F and a targeted replay shows that the minimum added context converts those misses without degrading unrelated review.

---

## Conditional experiment — C: precision-lens seat replacement

### Why it is not in the mainline sequence

A fourth general-purpose seat has low marginal contribution, so reallocating that seat is plausible. However, the evidence does not prove that a specialized lens fixes genuine capability misses. A successful lens could instead be closing a prompt gap, changing attention, or improving expression.

C is therefore an experiment, not a production recommendation.

### Preconditions

Run C only if:

1. A and E have been piloted;
2. a meaningful residual miss class remains;
3. the residual can be labeled without using third-party identifying data;
4. the trial can distinguish prompt/routing effects from capability limitations;
5. total seat count and coordinator disposition remain fixed.

### Promotion gate

Promote only if the lens produces net-new final-surviving catches while preserving the precision floor and retaining the useful contribution lost from the replaced seat.

### Coordinator conclusion

No coordinator-layer change is justified.

The S3 result is direct evidence that the disposition layer is successfully rejecting noise. Altering its drop, merge, severity, or transition rules would change the one layer demonstrated to be healthy while failing to address the measured upstream miss mechanisms.

Even B does not justify new coordinator semantics. At most, a later implementation may carry advisory examination metadata through the existing result path. It must not create automatic findings, coverage blocks, or transition authority.

## 6. Controlled pilots and falsifiers

## Common protocol

Use a small, pre-registered, anonymized fixed-input corpus containing:

- target-bucket cases for the lever;
- clean controls with no upheld finding;
- adjacent-bucket cases to detect mechanism leakage.

Run a paired or matched crossover:

1. baseline and treatment receive identical review inputs;
2. model and seat allocation remain fixed except when seat allocation is the tested lever;
3. run order is randomized where operationally possible;
4. adjudication is blinded to baseline/treatment identity;
5. test one lever at a time;
6. freeze coordinator disposition;
7. preserve aggregate seat count;
8. report per bucket and per seat rather than aggregate alone.

### Primary metric

- Final-surviving recall against upheld reference findings.

### Noise guardrails

- precision of final-surviving findings;
- unsupported or immaterial finding rate;
- positive-verification emission rate;
- duplicate emission rate;
- coordinator drop rate, interpreted alongside precision rather than alone;
- output/context cost.

Raw emitted-finding count is diagnostic only and cannot establish success.

### A falsifier — expression hygiene

**Prediction:** More previously buried real concerns become structured, final-surviving findings while positive-verification chatter decreases.

**Falsified if:**

- final-surviving recall does not improve;
- added findings are predominantly unsupported or immaterial;
- low-severity noise rises materially;
- the treatment merely converts tentative observations into findings.

**Rollback:** Restore the baseline expression contract and narrow the rule to higher-evidence cases.

### E falsifier — stakes allocation

**Prediction:** Routing high-stakes capability-bound classes to the strongest seat improves class-specific final-surviving recall at fixed seat count.

**Falsified if:**

- targeted recall is unchanged;
- overall precision falls;
- the displaced seat’s lost unique contribution equals or exceeds the routing gain;
- model concentration reduces independent corroboration without compensating gain.

**Rollback:** Restore baseline seat allocation; do not add another seat.

### B falsifier — changed-unit accounting

**Prediction:** Known skim misses become correctly reviewed or explicitly identifiable as unexamined.

**Falsified if:**

- accounting becomes boilerplate;
- output/context tax rises without target-bucket recall improvement;
- already-covered units receive less substantive attention;
- the leaf claims complete coverage while replay evidence still shows unexplained skim misses.

**Rollback:** Remove mandatory accounting or restrict it to larger/high-risk changes.

### F falsifier — narrow prompt-gap obligation

**Prediction:** One obligation recovers upheld findings in its targeted class without broad emission growth.

**Falsified if:**

- the new obligation produces mostly dropped or unsupported emissions;
- final-surviving recall remains unchanged;
- positive-verification or speculative output rises;
- gains occur only because A’s expression behavior was bundled into the same test.

**Rollback:** Remove that individual obligation. Do not retain it as general “awareness” wording.

### D falsifier — selective context

**Prediction:** Supplying the minimum missing contract or declaration context recovers context-absent cases without degrading unrelated review.

**Falsified if:**

- target cases remain missed;
- additional context causes more skim or unrelated speculation;
- token/context cost is disproportionate to observed recall gain;
- broad context performs no better than narrowly selected context.

**Rollback:** Return to baseline context; retain only deterministic selectors that individually pass.

### C falsifier — conditional precision lens

**Prediction:** A distinct lens produces net-new final-surviving residual catches beyond the contribution of the replaced seat.

**Falsified if:**

- it increases only raw emissions;
- precision falls below the baseline floor;
- its gains reproduce known prompt-gap cases rather than the targeted residual;
- the removed seat’s lost unique catches offset the gain;
- the experiment cannot distinguish specialization from routing or prompt effects.

**Disposition:** Do not promote unless the gain survives all precision and displacement guards.

## 7. Recommendation

Adopt the following as the operator-sign-off candidate:

1. **A first:** expression hygiene in the shared static leaf prompt.
2. **E second:** fixed-count stakes allocation to the strongest seat.
3. **B third:** bounded changed-unit accounting.
4. **F fourth:** individually validated prompt-gap obligations.
5. **D last:** selective context expansion only for a demonstrated residual.
6. **C experimental only:** precision-gated shadow replacement after A and E.

Explicitly reject:

- coordinator or disposition redesign;
- increased blind-spot detection volume;
- adding a fifth seat;
- broad full-context expansion;
- treating all solo catches as coverage failures;
- treating prompt specialization as a proven cure for genuine capability limits.
